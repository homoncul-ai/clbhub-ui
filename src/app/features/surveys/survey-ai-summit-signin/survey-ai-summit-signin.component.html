Greater Haverhill Chamber of Commerce

Haverhill AI Summit

Responsible Preparation for the AI Economy

We're collecting information to gauge interest and plan for community needs. Please take a few minutes to share your background and interests so we can connect you with relevant opportunities from the summit and beyond.

The Greater Haverhill Chamber of Commerce has announced the Haverhill AI Summit, an event designed to bring together workers, students, educators, businesses, and community leaders to prepare for the AI economy responsibly and practically. Rather than focusing on fear, the summit will emphasize awareness, preparation, and opportunity for the region.

"Artificial Intelligence is already changing the way we work, learn, hire, manufacture, and compete. The risk is not AI taking away jobs; it's being outcompeted by those who know how to use it. That is why this summit matters."
— City Council Vice President John Michitson

Key themes

  • AI workforce readiness
  • Education and business integration
  • Responsible AI use
  • Small business adoption
  • Advanced manufacturing applications
  • Economic opportunity and community readiness

Featured speakers

  • Professor Scott Latham, University of Massachusetts Lowell — keynote on how innovation and AI are transforming industries, organizations, and jobs.
  • Dan Sarofian-Butin, Merrimack College — education, workforce, and business integration.
  • Perry Bailes, Support Forge LLC — practical AI integration for small businesses and advanced manufacturing.
  • Seth Cordes, Uncommon Catalyst — ethics, trust, cybersecurity, and responsible AI.

The summit is expected to showcase clbhub.org, the collaborative workforce and career pathway platform connecting workers, students, employers, educators, and training opportunities into one ecosystem. Organizers envision the event as the start of ongoing collaboration, with planned outcomes including monthly cohorts and face-to-face working groups focused on AI learning, workforce transition, business adoption, education collaboration, and community readiness.

"Technology alone will not determine success. Collaboration, relationships, and shared learning will. The communities that prepare together will be the communities that succeed together."
— John Michitson
