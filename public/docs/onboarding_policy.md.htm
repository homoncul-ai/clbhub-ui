# Onboarding Policy for CLB Hub ## Motivation ### The Problem We're Solving CLB Hub is an application that maps students' needs for employment and educational opportunities with what's available in their local neighborhood. The platform: - **Restricts searches geographically** to a reasonable radius from the student's location - **Matches vocational interests** with available opportunities (e.g., a student interested in plumbing is matched with plumbers seeking interns) - **Targets underserved populations** including students who may be struggling academically, non-native English speakers, and those who lack traditional pathways to opportunity ### Target Population - **Age Range:** Students between 14 years old and high school graduation - **Demographics:** Underserved populations, including Spanish speakers and students not thriving in traditional academic settings - **Location:** Massachusetts (initial focus) ### Why We Need This Policy When building an application that serves minors, we must address several critical concerns: 1. **Legal Compliance:** Different age groups have different privacy requirements and consent needs 2. **Safety:** Applications catering to children attract bad actors who self-select into these spaces 3. **Trust:** We must be able to demonstrate due diligence to parents, school committees, state officials, and insurance providers 4. **Privacy:** Students at different ages have varying needs for privacy and interaction restrictions (e.g., parental consent, parent presence in chats) --- ## Strawman Policy ### 1. Age-Based Privacy and Consent Tiers Students should be categorized by age with appropriate safeguards: | Age Group | Consent Requirements | Communication Rules | |-----------|---------------------|---------------------| | 14-15 | Full parental consent required | Parent must be included in all chats | | 16-17 | Parental consent required | Parent has access to view all communications | | 18+ | Self-consent | Standard adult communication | *Note: These tiers need validation against Massachusetts state law and federal regulations (COPPA, etc.)* ### 2. Provider Onboarding Requirements To vet providers and ensure legitimacy, we propose requiring: - **In-Person Verification:** A representative from the provider organization must appear in person during onboarding - **Identity Documentation:** Collection of name, contact information, and identification - **Organizational Validation:** - Active website or verifiable online presence - Tax return from the previous year (to prevent fly-by-night operations) - Business registration or 501(c)(3) documentation - **Background Checks:** Determine if CORI (Criminal Offender Record Information) checks are required for providers who will interact with minors ### 3. Scope Restrictions for Opportunities Not all opportunities are appropriate for minors. We should establish: - **Approved Categories:** List of acceptable vocational categories (e.g., plumbing, electrical, healthcare, technology, culinary arts) - **Prohibited Categories:** Venues or roles inappropriate for minors (e.g., bars, adult entertainment venues) - **Environment Standards:** Requirements for safe, supervised work environments ### 4. Provider Training Requirements Providers should complete training that covers: - How to appropriately interact with interns and students - What behavior is acceptable and unacceptable - Legal requirements when working with minors - Reporting obligations and communication boundaries **Open Questions:** - What accreditation should the training provider have? - Should completion of training be a prerequisite for posting opportunities? - Does training completion help mitigate liability? ### 5. Onboarding Team Requirements The team of students/staff who onboard providers should have: - **Training:** Standard curriculum on vetting procedures and red flags - **Supervision:** Oversight from adult staff members - **Documentation Skills:** Ability to properly collect and verify required documents - **Communication Training:** How to represent the organization professionally ### 6. Working with State and Local Officials To operate under official auspices and build credibility: - Partner with local school districts and vocational schools - Coordinate with Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary Education - Explore partnerships with workforce development boards - Investigate state programs that support youth employment and internships - Consider MOU (Memorandum of Understanding) agreements with municipalities --- ## Next Steps 1. Validate age-tier requirements against Massachusetts law 2. Research CORI requirements for youth-serving organizations 3. Draft provider training curriculum outline 4. Identify potential state/local partners 5. Consult with legal counsel on liability mitigation --- *This document is a working draft intended to be refined through stakeholder feedback and legal review.*
