# CCL Hub: Connecting Communities Locally ## Overview **CCL Hub** is a community-centered digital platform designed to map and activate the ecosystem of a town or region. Its core purpose is to help residents—especially students—connect with local institutions, businesses, and nonprofits to access opportunities, services, and vocational programs. The platform promotes collaboration between individuals and local providers by making the town’s social and economic network visible and interactive online. ## Initial Mission The project's first focus is tackling Massachusetts' significant backlog of students seeking admission to vocational schools. **CCL Hub** aims to bridge that gap by connecting students, schools, and training providers so that young people can discover, apply to, and prepare for local programs that align with their interests and career goals. ## Key Roles and Participants The platform supports multiple user roles to model real community relationships: - **Clients (Students and Families):** Students use the Hub to explore local opportunities, build resumes, and express career interests. - **Providers (Schools, Colleges, Businesses, Nonprofits):** Providers maintain catalogs of offerings—such as classes, internships, events, or services—that students can browse and apply for. - **Advisors (Guidance Counselors and Mentors):** Advisors oversee student progress, engage in secure chat discussions, and monitor work requests for safety and accountability. ## Core Features 1. **Community Directory and Onboarding** The Hub captures the digital presence of local organizations and institutions, enabling students to easily find verified local providers. Schools facilitate onboarding for students and families. 2. **Provider Catalogs** Each provider maintains a catalog of offerings (courses, jobs, events, or services). Students can browse these and submit "work requests" (tickets) to engage directly with providers. 3. **Personal Goals and Interest Matching** Students set up personal goals (e.g., "I want to be a plumber") that serve as context for personalized search and matching. The system uses vocational classifications to suggest relevant local opportunities aligned with those interests. 4. **Showing Interest** Students find offerings either by searching or via a personalized feed. Each offering includes a thumbs-up button, and clicking it creates an "Interest" so the student can quickly save many items for further pursuit. 5. **Work Request System** After the Interest stage, students can proceed to signup, review the requirements in a "Signup Packet," prepare a resume, and complete any forms. When signup is complete, a work request (ticket) is created and routed to the provider, where agents review profiles, resumes, and transcripts before responding; ticket types can range from course enrollment to resource requests like transportation or school materials. 6. **Communication and Safety** A built-in group chat system, modeled after Slack, allows students to communicate securely with providers and advisors. Conversations are visible to the advisory team to ensure compliance and safety, especially for students under 18. 7. **Student Profiles and Resume Builder** Students can build goal-specific resumes tailored to vocational interests. These resumes support applications and structured interactions with providers. 8. **Tracking Progress Toward Goals** Each personal goal has an advisory team that may include guidance counselors, parents, mentors, and the student. This team collaborates around that goal using tickets, chat, and shared information. 9. **Analytics and Reporting** Providers gain insight into community needs—such as which programs or careers attract the most interest—helping them shape future offerings. Students can also view statistics about programs to inform their choices. 10. **General Research and Information Packets** Beyond matching and tickets, the platform curates relevant career information that can be maintained by the community. AI can assist with initial curation, while advocates and providers refine and share "Information Packets" with students. ## Philosophy: The Local Ecosystem At the heart of **CCL Hub** is the idea of locality—anchoring digital engagement to real communities. By focusing on local access and proximity, the platform ensures that opportunities are practical and attainable for students. This ecosystem approach aims to strengthen community ties, enhance equity in educational access, and empower youth to navigate their career paths within a network of supportive local partners. ## Data Stewardship and Intent **CCL Hub** is not intended to be a for-profit enterprise; the goal is simply to solve real community problems. The project pledges that data from this platform will never be sold.
